Method

This reproduces the study dcposch published on 16 Aug 2026, extended to the full wFiat family and its direct LATAM competitors. For every coin, on every chain it is deployed to, we buy it with the chain's dollar stablecoin at the best available route, then sell the coins we received straight back. The round trip absorbs both spreads, both fee tiers and all slippage — which is why the honest number is always negative.

Routes come from two places. Venue quoters (Uniswap v3 and v4, Aerodrome and Velodrome SlipStream, Pancake v3) are called directly for every fee tier against every plausible dollar leg — native USDC, bridged USDC.e, USDT, DAI, cUSD — because quoting only native USDC reports "no liquidity" for coins that trade fine against a bridged dollar. Pools those quoters cannot reach are priced from raw pool state instead: a local reimplementation of the v3 swap, verified against the on-chain quoter to the wei, plus constant-product and solidly-style maths. That is how pools on forks with no quoter — a newer Aerodrome CL factory on Base, Uniswap v3 on Gnosis — get counted rather than silently dropped.

Coverage is not limited to coins an indexer already knows about. Twin's coins appear on no token list, price feed or DEX aggregator. Their published registry covers only Base and Polygon and was last touched the day before Arbitrum minting began, so it misses where almost all of the float actually sits. The Arbitrum contracts were recovered instead by deriving Twin's deployer from the CREATE addresses of two known tokens and enumerating its nonce sequence, which turns up ten currencies — the seven live ones plus Paraguay, Uruguay and Venezuela, deployed and still unissued. Every address was verified on-chain for name, symbol, decimals and supply. Their result — no route at any size, on any venue, against any dollar — is a measurement, not a gap in the data.

What to trust, and what not to

  • This is a snapshot, not a month. The original sampled hourly for 30 days; this is one pass at current block. A pool that was deep last week and is empty today reads as empty here — which is the correct answer for a payment you want to make now, and the wrong one for judging a coin's typical depth.
  • On-chain only. Aggregators often fill better by routing to market makers off-chain, and those fills leave no pool state to read. Coins whose real volume is OTC — A7A5 is the clearest case — look worse here than they trade.
  • Supply is summed across all chains. The original appears to have used single-chain supply for some rows, which is why its ARS float reads roughly ten times smaller than the on-chain total. Ratios here are correspondingly stricter.
  • Pool TVL counts both sides. Reserve figures come from pool discovery and include the dollar leg and the coin leg together, so the dollar actually available to sell into is roughly half the number shown.
